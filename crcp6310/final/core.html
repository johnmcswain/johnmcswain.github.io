NOAA CO-OPS · six-minute water level

Three days of one gauge,
laid down as strata.

Time runs downward. Every six minutes deposits a layer, and its color and grain come from how far the sea ran from its prediction. Quiet water settles fine and dark. A storm leaves a coarse bright band you can find years later.

Reading the coreOldest layer at the top, most recent at the bottom. Fine dark grain is water behaving. Coarse warm grain is water arriving faster or later than the moon accounted for.
The left marginThe prediction still draws as vector, now as a narrow vertical ribbon. The sediment never touches it. The gap between the two columns is the whole subject.
Flow fieldThe same three days as a fluid. Tidal phase sets the direction, residual sets the turbulence. Slack water combs flat. Surge tears it into vortices.
Tidal timeOn, one second of watching is roughly ten minutes of ocean, so the piece changes slower than you expect. Off, it deposits fast.
