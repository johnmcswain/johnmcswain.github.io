Statement of Professional Goals

My professional path has been shaped by a conviction that emerging technologies achieve their greatest impact when guided by principled instructional design and grounded in learning science. Across two decades working at the intersection of technology development and education, as a software architect, applied researcher, curriculum developer, and university instructor, I have pursued several interrelated goals in Learning Design and Technologies.

Continuous Learning and Mastery of Emerging Technologies

I engage with emerging technologies as a lifelong learner, focused not just on how systems work, but on how they reshape learning and work environments. My graduate studies across technical and human-centered fields reflect a purposeful effort to see technology from multiple perspectives. Pursuing an M.Ed. in Learning Design and Technologies at the University of South Carolina has deepened my understanding that true expertise means connecting technical skill with a solid foundation in adult learning theory and instructional design. In my current role as a solutions architect, I focus on enterprise AI systems that are transforming how organizations learn and grow. Supporting clients in both industry and academia, I care deeply about not just implementing new technologies, but understanding how they impact adult learning and expertise. My central questions are: How do AI-powered environments change the way people build knowledge? What supports help learners understand both the possibilities and limits of AI? How can we design learning that builds critical thinking as well as technical skill? These questions guide my work at the intersection of technology and education.

Designing and Delivering Effective Instruction

I believe thoughtful instructional design can changes lives. Since 2017, I've taught hundreds of students at Georgia Tech's College of Lifelong Learning and Scheller College of Business, using evidence-based methods to improve learning. When students struggled with key concepts, I redesigned my course using cognitive load theory and worked examples, leading to higher grades and greater satisfaction. This experience confirmed for me that systematic design makes a real difference. My commitment to evidence-based teaching reaches beyond the classroom. At AWS, I created the ML Essentials lab series for over 10,000 learners, building pathways from basics to advanced skills. At Trilogy Education Services, I used learning analytics to identify at-risk students and personalize support, which improved engagement and completion across 15 universities.These experiences have fueled my passion for advancing the scholarship of teaching and learning, especially as technology blurs the lines between tool and tutor. I hope to contribute new insights about instructional design in these evolving contexts.

Mentoring Learners Through Reasoning and Motivation

Mentorship matters as much as instruction. The most meaningful teaching moments often happen in one-on-one conversations, helping students navigate career shifts or supporting professionals in new technical fields. I believe mentorship answers questions content alone can't: Why pursue this path? How does one stay motivated? What makes learning genuinely meaningful? I draw on research in motivation and my own unconventional academic journey, five graduate degrees and a range of career transitions, to guide learners whose paths aren't traditional. Whether advising students from underrepresented backgrounds or those changing careers, I aim to provide practical guidance and encouragement when the way forward feels unclear.
