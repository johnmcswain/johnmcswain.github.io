I know these three references well, and there's a real technical fork hiding in them that we should settle before writing anything.

The references aren't one style — they're two

  • Marvel vs Capcom 2 is true pixel art — hand-pixeled sprites, limited palette, a visible pixel grid, chunky comic-book coloring. Circa-2000 sprite craft.
  • BlazBlue: Calamity Trigger is ultra-high-res hand-drawn 2D — smooth anime sprites, bold clean linework, soft cel shading. No visible pixels at all.
  • DNF Duel is 3D-rendered-to-look-2D — the Guilty Gear Xrd pipeline: 3D models with hand-tuned normals, thick deliberate outlines, painterly textures, dramatic rim light.

So “pixel perfect” plus those three is internally contradictory — two of the three are deliberately not pixels. My read is you mean “crisp, clean, artifact-free, high-fidelity,” and that the BlazBlue/DNF cel-shaded look is your real target.

One caveat: diffusion models render the cel-shaded anime-fighter look superbly, but they're genuinely bad at true pixel art — they fake it with smeared pseudo-pixels that aren't on a real grid. If you actually want MvC2-grade sprites, that's a dedicated pixel pipeline or a downscale/cleanup pass, not a one-shot prompt.

Platform steer

For this aesthetic, Midjourney is the stronger tool. Higgsfield's consistency engine (Soul ID) is built around training a persistent identity from real photos — tuned for photoreal AI-influencer/video work, and it will tug a stylized cel-shaded 2D character toward realism. Midjourney V7 with Niji 7 (their anime model, released Jan 2026) is squarely in the target style.

What makes a good anchor for latent variants

Built for clarity, not drama: full-body, front or 3/4 view, even neutral-but-characterful pose, flat plain background, even lighting, and 3–4 strong distinctive identifiers (signature color, silhouette, weapon/costume motif, hair shape) — those are what the model latches onto across generations.

I had the reference mechanism wrong for Niji, and the error pinpoints why. --oref, --ow, and the old --cref are Omni Reference, which is V7-only, and Niji 7 doesn't support character/object reference at all — so there's no true character-lock on Niji 7. Scrap the Omni Reference variant kit for the Niji path.

What actually works on Niji 7

The uploaded image acts as an image prompt, dialed with --iw (image weight), not --ow. On Niji 7 that runs 0–2 (default 1); at --iw 2 you hold pose, vibe, and colors most tightly. Niji 7 also supports --sref (style reference).

The catch: with no identity lock, the text must carry consistency — put her full fixed description (hair, costume, palette) in every prompt.

Corrected variant template (upload anchor as image prompt):

natural skin tone, near-black olive-brown hair in a braided tied-up style,

Arc System Works style, cel-shaded, bold clean linework, plain flat cool light-grey background
--iw 2 --niji 7 --ar 2:3

Same [POSE] swaps as before; optionally add --sref <anchor-URL> to lock the style.

The fork (consistency vs. aesthetic)

  • Stay on Niji 7: best anime look, identity wobbles. Mitigate with --iw 2, full description every time, cherry-pick.
  • Switch variants to V7 + Omni Reference: real character lock. (Correction: --ow default is 100, practical range ~50–250; above 400–500 degrades — “500” was too hot.) V7's base look is less anime-specialized.
  • Hybrid (recommended): generate the anchor in Niji 7, run variants in V7 with --oref <niji-anchor>. Omni Reference accepts external images, so the Niji look carries into V7's stronger consistency. One model switch between anchor and variants.
