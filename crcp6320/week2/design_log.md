### 14:58 — Parallax & design log requested
**Input (user):**
Add parallax for emotional impact. Generate `design_log.md` summarising the dialogue with timestamps, inputs, outputs.

**Output (designer):**
Added layered scroll-driven parallax using a single requestAnimationFrame loop and CSS custom properties — no external libraries.

Architecture:
- **Global hero parallax** — body sets `--scroll-y`, hero SVG drifts down at 0.18× scroll, hero text counter-drifts up at -0.08× (depth illusion: diagram recedes, words rise toward reader)
- **Section-scoped parallax** — each `<section>` gets a `--rel` value from -1.2 to 1.2 representing its position relative to viewport centre. Elements inside use `calc(var(--rel) * Npx)` to drift at element-specific rates
- **Featured product image** drifts -42px through viewport with subtle scale, creating a "the unit floats" feel against the dark backdrop
- **Founder's quote** rises -32px while its meta column stays put — the words come into focus
- **Catalogue glyphs and facility city names** get gentle drift (-10px to -12px) — editorial poster motion
- **Section titles** drift -16px relative to their eyebrows — typographic depth

Scroll-reveal system:
- `.reveal` class — fade + 24px rise, 1.1s cubic-bezier easing
- `.reveal-stagger` — children animate in with 100ms cascading delays (4 children supported)
- IntersectionObserver fires once per element, then unobserves (no continuous overhead)
- Threshold 0.12 with rootMargin -8% — elements reveal as they meaningfully enter, not edge-cases

Accessibility:
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` short-circuits everything — all reveals immediately set to `.in`, all transforms killed via `!important`. The static layout is complete and intentional; motion is enhancement only

Performance:
- Single rAF loop throttles scroll updates
- `will-change: transform` on the elements that actually animate
- IntersectionObserver replaces scroll listeners for reveal logic
- Resize throttled to 100ms debounce
- No layout thrashing — only reads geometry once per frame inside rAF

### 15:08 — Dark theme added
**Input (user):**
Provide a toggle between light and dark themes.

**Output (designer):**
Built a full dual-theme system framed as **DAY EDITION** / **NIGHT EDITION** — the in-world conceit being that this is a real industrial publication with a day and a night printing. The dark theme is not a different brand; it is the same catalogue, after hours.

Architecture decisions:

**Semantic tokens.** Refactored the palette to introduce semantic tokens (`--bg`, `--bg-alt`, `--bg-inverse`, `--fg`, `--fg-mute`, `--fg-on-inv`, `--accent`, `--accent-on-inv`) that resolve to different raw values in each theme. The featured product section and the footer use `--bg-inverse` — they remain the deepest moments on the page in *both* themes, just at different absolute depths.

**Dark palette.**
- `--bone` (page background) becomes `#14202c` — deep ink, never pure black
- `--ink` (foreground) becomes `#ebe5d6` — the bone we used as background in light mode
- `--patina` lifts to `#6b9a98` for contrast on dark
- `--alarm` warms slightly to `#e86a3a`
- `--bg-inverse` drops to `#0a0f15` — even deeper than the page, so featured/footer remain visually deeper inversions
- The patina (oxidised copper) and cadmium orange remain the brand anchors across both modes

**Anti-flash script.** An inline script in `<head>` reads `localStorage` and `prefers-color-scheme` *before* the page renders, setting `data-theme="dark"` on `<html>` if needed. This eliminates flash-of-wrong-theme on load — critical because a cream-then-dark flash would feel cheap.

**The toggle component.** A minimal monoline switch with sun and moon SVG icons, positioned top-right at 14px/28px offset. A sliding knob moves left/right on click. Below the switch, a micro-label in mono reads **DAY EDITION** or **NIGHT EDITION** — this is the brand voice doing its work on a UI element that is normally generic. The label hides on mobile.

**SVG handling.** The hero diagram, featured product silhouette, and cascade diagram all contain hardcoded `#1a2332` and `#ebe5d6` color values. Rather than refactor each SVG (risking breaking the carefully composed compositions), I added scoped CSS overrides in dark mode that swap colors via attribute selectors (`[fill="#1a2332"] { fill: #ebe5d6 !important; }`). This keeps the SVG source pristine — the diagrams are art, and editing them is dangerous.

**Persistence.**
- `localStorage` key: `tw-theme` — values `"light"` or `"dark"`
- First visit: defaults to system preference via `prefers-color-scheme: dark`
- System preference changes are followed *only if* the user hasn't made an explicit choice
- Once the user clicks the toggle, their choice persists across sessions

**Transition.** Background and color transitions on `body` and other key elements are 350ms ease — fast enough to feel intentional, slow enough to feel deliberate. The noise overlay opacity also transitions (0.035 → 0.025) so the texture sits correctly in both modes.

**Featured section in dark mode.** The featured product section's inner image panel drops to `#020608` — nearly black, so the brushed-steel MFPU silhouette still has somewhere to float against. The manifesto section's bone-warm background also flips: in dark mode it's `#1a2733`, a lifted card tone that distinguishes it from the surrounding page.

The result: clicking the toggle feels like turning the page from the day printing to the night printing. The hairlines, the typography, the diagrams, the patina accents — everything holds its identity. The cream becomes ink, the ink becomes cream, but the editorial voice never wavers.

### 15:25 — E-commerce simulation and 3D layered schematic
**Input (user):**
The site needs to *feel* like an e-commerce site — add-to-cart, product detail screens, checkout flow. Use localStorage to persist between sessions. The featured product should have a 3D schematic effect that rotates and expands as the user scrolls, working in concert with the parallax.

**Output (designer):**

This is the last load-bearing piece of the experience. Two distinct challenges, each requiring careful framing.

**Framing decision: the shopping flow must remain in-character.** Option A is design fiction — no real commerce. But it must *feel* shoppable. The solution: every interaction reads as the kind of fictional industrial-procurement flow a Stuttgart customer would experience in 1987, not Amazon.

- The cart is the **dossier**. Never "cart."
- The button is **Add to Dossier**, never "Add to Cart."
- The checkout requests a **Formal Quotation**, never "Checkout" or "Place Order."
- The confirmation is a brief acknowledgement letter signed by **Margarethe Holzer, Senior Sales Engineer**, with a reference number formatted TW-YYYY-NNNN.

Every shopper interaction is therefore a piece of the world. The user can absolutely shop — they just shop the way a procurement officer would.

**E-commerce system architecture:**

*Product catalogue.* Four products fully wired (MFPU-7400, Sophist Standard, DRP-12 Premise Forge, BQA No. 0247) with designation, name, line, price, lead time, lede copy, full spec table, and a small mono glyph for dossier line entries. Defined as a `PRODUCTS` constant in the script.

*Dossier indicator.* Top-right button — mono type, hairline border, **DOSSIER · NN** with the count badge filling cadmium orange when non-empty. Adding an item pulses the badge with a spring-back animation (scale 1 → 1.5 → 1, 500ms, cubic-bezier 0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1).

*Slide-in drawer.* 480px wide (full-width mobile), translates from `100%` → `0` on open, 450ms cubic-bezier. Structured as a procurement document: header with **DOSSIER** title and *Configuration · TW-2026* submeta, body with line entries (glyph + designation + reference + lead + price + quantity stepper + remove), footer with mono totals (*Subtotal · Calibration · Stuttgart [included] · VAT 7.7% (CH) · Quotation Total*) and the primary action **REQUEST FORMAL QUOTATION →**.

The *Calibration · Stuttgart · included* line is the deadpan punching through the e-commerce furniture. Nobody else's checkout has that line.

*Toast notification.* Bottom-centre, slides up with spring-back. Inverse-bg, mono type, hairline patina-bordered checkmark, reference number in patina accent. Auto-dismisses after 3.2 seconds. Copy format: *"MFPU-7400 added to dossier — TW-4729"*.

*Product detail overlay.* Slides up from the bottom (translateY 100% → 0), 550ms. Top sticky bar with breadcrumb and close. Two-column hero with text/specs/CTA left, custom SVG patent diagram right against the inverse-bg. Each of the four products has its own schematic — the MFPU shows the rack chassis with patina sphere, Sophist shows the desktop unit with display, DRP-12 shows the industrial forge with temperature readout, BQA shows the brass-and-walnut heritage apparatus.

*Checkout overlay.* Same slide-up pattern. Renders the §6 configuration form prefilled with a summary of dossier contents. Form has fields for name, organisation, role, country, deployment context (radio), throughput, power infrastructure, competing equipment, and notes. Submission generates a TW-YYYY-NNNN reference, hides the form, and shows the **Margarethe Holzer** confirmation letter. The dossier is cleared on successful submission — the user has filed their request.

*Persistence.* `localStorage` under key `tw-dossier`, shape `[{id, qty}]`. Loaded on page load. Returning users see a *Dossier restored · N items* toast 1.2s after page load — a small touch that recognises them.

*Keyboard support.* Product cards are `tabindex="0"` and respond to Enter/Space. Escape closes any open overlay in priority order (checkout → detail → drawer). Click-outside via the scrim closes the drawer.

*Validation.* The quotation form requires name, organisation, country, and a deployment context selection. Missing fields scroll into view and pulse their bottom border in cadmium orange for 2 seconds — feedback without breaking the deadpan.

**3D layered schematic — the difficult problem.**

The risk: going too far into rendered 3D kills the satire. Patent illustrations are *not* photorealistic. The right move is a **2.5D parallactic exploded blueprint** — the diagram stays in the hairline patent-illustration register, but its layers separate in depth as you scroll.

*Implementation.* Replaced the single featured SVG with six absolutely-positioned layer divs inside a `.schematic-scene` element with `transform-style: preserve-3d` and a parent `.schematic-stage` with `perspective: 2400px`. Each layer is a separate SVG element with its own `translateZ` value.

Layer depths back-to-front:
- `layer-sphere` z: -180px — the patina accent sphere (deepest)
- `layer-grid` z: -120px — background hairline grid
- `layer-chassis` z: -40px — rack body and shadow
- `layer-front-panel` z: +20px — ventilation, display, LEDs
- `layer-cartridges` z: +60px — hot-swap bays (project most forward)
- `layer-annotations` z: +140px — fig. 2 callouts and leader lines

*Drivers.* The existing per-section `--rel` parallax variable (already swept -1.2 to 1.2 as the section travels through viewport) drives two transforms on the scene:

```css
transform: rotateY(calc(var(--rel) * 14deg)) rotateX(calc(var(--rel) * 4deg));
```

And each layer's translateZ is augmented by an explode factor (`--explode: calc(var(--rel) * -1)`):

```css
.layer-cartridges {
  transform: translateZ(calc(60px + var(--explode) * 80px));
}
```

The result: when the section is centred in viewport, the unit assembles into a coherent patent diagram. As it enters and exits viewport, the layers separate by up to 220px of additional depth, exposing the internal structure. Simultaneously the whole scene rotates ±14° on Y and ±4° on X, so the unit appears to turn slowly on an isometric pedestal.

*Perspective choice.* 2400px keeps the rotation gentle — at 1000px it becomes a fisheye effect, at 5000px the depth disappears. 2400px lands in the *"blueprint coming alive on the page"* register.

*Reduced motion fallback.* Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, both `.schematic-scene` and all `.layer` elements have their transforms killed (`transform: none !important; translateZ(0) !important;`). The static composition is complete — all six layers visible, stacked correctly via their natural z-order, with the patent diagram fully assembled. The user sees the canonical schematic without any motion.

*What makes this work satirically.* The diagram never becomes a product render. It remains hairline strokes, patent-illustration callouts, mono dimension labels, and the *FIG. 2 — FLAGSHIP REFERENCE* corner annotation. The 3D is *suggestion* — the depth of an exploded blueprint, not the depth of a photograph. The MFPU-7400 never becomes real. It just looks more like a piece of equipment you might find in a Sulzer service manual from 1971.

*Architectural decision: it's a plate, not a wallpaper.*

Most design-fiction sites collapse when they reach for stock photography — the illusion dissolves. The instinct is to wallpaper the hero or fade an image behind text. The right move here is to give the photograph its own real estate: a **numbered editorial plate** between the hero and the catalogue, treated exactly the way a printed annual report or catalogue would treat a centrefold photograph. Numbered. Captioned. Dated. Sitting between sections of text the way a printed plate sits between gathered signatures.

This is more honest to the conceit (it *is* a catalogue, and catalogues have plates) and more useful compositionally — the existing hero is already doing precise diagrammatic work, and putting a photo behind it would muddy both.

*The plate section.*

- Full-bleed 100vw × 88vh, between hero and catalogue, presented as **Plate I**
- Hairline rules top and bottom, four hairline corner brackets at 60% opacity
- Top-left plate label (*Plate — I, Catalogue 2026*) in heavy condensed
- Top-right reference data (*Photographed Spring 1987 · archive · TW · 1987.03.114*) in mono
- Bottom-left caption: mono eyebrow with leading hairline rule → heavy condensed display title (*Reichenbachstrasse 12* with italic serif emphasis on the number) → body-serif italic byline → mono metadata grid (Opened / Architect / Staff / Output per annum)
- CSS overlay: radial vignette + subtle top/bottom gradients lock any source image into the palette regardless of raw variation
- Parallax: image drifts at `calc(--rel * 80px)` while caption drifts at `calc(--rel * -24px)` — opposing motion creates depth
- Image element extends `-10%` above/below visible plate (`height: 120%`) so parallax never reveals edges
- Fallback: 40×40px hairline grid pattern over deep ink background appears when no image loaded — page never looks broken during iteration

*Image specifications.*

- **Dimensions:** **2880 × 1800 px** (16:10 aspect)
- **Native generation:** Z-Image-Turbo (verified working — current asset produced with this) at official Text-to-Image workflow defaults. Fallbacks: SDXL at 1536 × 960, Flux at 1344 × 832, upscaled 2× via 4x-UltraSharp ESRGAN or SUPIR
- **Format:** PNG (lossless). Expect 6–12 MB at full resolution.

A complete prompt package: positive prompt, negative prompt, recommended models (Z-Image-Turbo verified working; Flux.1 Dev and SDXL with Juggernaut XL v9 / RealVisXL v4 as fallbacks), sampler settings, and an **eight-version iteration strategy**.

### 16:05 — Plate text legibility scrim
**Input (user):**
Screenshots showed that text overlaid on the photographic plate had insufficient contrast in both themes. In dark mode the *Reichenbachstrasse 12* title disappeared into the building's shadow entirely.

**Output (designer):**

Two root causes identified:

1. Plate text inherited theme variables — `color: var(--bone)` resolved to cream in light mode but to deep ink in dark mode, exactly the opposite of what was needed since the plate photograph is dark in *both* themes.
2. The image's mid-tones were the same brightness as the text. The existing centered-vignette overlay darkened the wrong areas — the middle, not the corners where text actually sits.

Fix, two-part:

**Locked plate text to bone always.** Every plate text element (`plate-eyebrow`, `plate-title`, `plate-title em`, `plate-byline`, `plate-meta`, `plate-meta .row span:last-child`, `plate-label`, `plate-label .num`, `plate-ref`, `plate-ref strong`, `plate-caption` parent, `plate-frame .corner`) now uses the hardcoded `#ebe5d6` value instead of `var(--bone)`. The plate is a dark-island within both themes — its text must remain cream in both editions. This is an intentional override of the cross-theme system, justified by the fact that the photograph beneath does not change between themes.

**Added a four-corner radial scrim.** A new `.plate-scrim` element sits between `.plate-image` and the text layers (`z-index: 1`). It contains four radial gradients in `rgba(15, 22, 32, 0.72)` ink-navy, each anchored to a corner, fading to fully transparent by ~40% of the way to center. The result: the four text zones get a subtle ink darkening, while the building's center stays completely clear. In dark mode the scrim deepens to `rgba(5, 8, 14, 0.82)` since the surrounding theme is already darker. The scrim does not parallax — it stays anchored to the viewport, so text-darkening remains where text is regardless of image drift.

**Three smaller refinements:**
- Added subtle ink-toned text-shadows to all plate text (`0 2px 12px rgba(15, 22, 32, 0.55)` on the title, lighter on smaller text). Invisible at casual inspection but defeats the busy-background problem at the edges of each letterform.
- Raised base opacities slightly (byline 0.75 → 0.92, meta 0.7 → 0.88, eyebrow 0.8 → 0.85).
- Strengthened the meta block's top border (0.3 → 0.45 alpha) and the corner brackets (0.6 → 0.7 opacity) since the scrim around them now gives them somewhere to live.

The plate now reads cleanly in both editions. The building's center remains unobstructed. The text shadows are tuned to disappear under casual inspection. The cream-text-over-dark-photograph identity is now consistent across both themes — which is honest, since the photograph itself doesn't actually change between themes.

### 16:18 — Ambient audio control
**Input (user):**
Add a background audio loop with mute and volume controls that persist similar to the theme and dossier buttons. The file is `calm_wonder.mp3`.

**Output (designer):**

Framed in the brand voice as the **Ambient track** — an editorial choice, not "background music" or "soundtrack." A magazine that ships with a vinyl insert.

**Hard rule: off by default.** First visit, the page is silent. Autoplay-with-sound is hostile design (and browsers block it anyway). The user opts in.

**The control component.** Slots into the existing top-right cluster as the third persistent control. Left-to-right order: ambient (`right: 220px`) → dossier (`right: 90px`) → theme (`right: 28px`). Same visual register as the theme toggle: hairline border, mono micro-type, monoline icon, a label beneath reading **Ambient · On** or **Ambient · Off**.

The mute toggle uses an **animated three-bar waveform icon**:
- When playing: the three bars pulse vertically via CSS keyframes (0.9s loop, staggered 0/0.2/0.4s delays). The motion is subtle but constant — visual confirmation that audio is active.
- When muted: bars compress to a single 1.5px hairline at 45% opacity.

A **hover-revealed volume slider** slides out to the right of the toggle, animating `max-width` from 0 to 120px over 350ms. The slider uses thin hairline styling matching the rest of the design system — 1px track, 8×12px sharp-cornered ink-filled thumb, no rounded edges. Cross-browser styled (WebKit + Firefox).

**Persistence.** Stored in `localStorage` under the key `tw-ambient` as a JSON object: `{ playing: boolean, volume: number (0–100) }`. Default volume: 40%. Survives sessions, refreshes, tab switches.

**Resume on return.** If a returning visitor had ambient ON in their previous session, the page attempts to resume playback 800ms after load. If the browser blocks the resume (no user gesture in current session), the state is silently reverted to OFF and the UI updates to match — no error, no nag, no "click to enable audio" banner. The graceful failure mode is itself the design.

**Volume behaviour.** Sliding to 0 does NOT auto-mute the toggle. The user remains in "ON" state at zero volume, so unsliding restores audibility without re-toggling. This matches the convention of audio applications (Spotify, Apple Music) where the play/pause and volume controls are independent concepts.

**Error handling.** If the audio file is missing, fails to load, or throws any decode error, the control silently reverts to OFF state. The page does not break. The control behaves as though the user clicked it to mute.

**Accessibility.**
- Dynamic `aria-label` on toggle ("Play ambient audio" / "Mute ambient audio")
- `aria-label="Ambient audio volume"` on the slider
- `:focus-within` reveals the volume slider — keyboard users get the same experience as mouse users
- The label beneath provides a redundant text indicator of state
- Audio is never autoplayed without explicit user action

**LocalStorage state map.** The page now persists three independent keys, none affecting the others:
- `tw-theme` — light/dark preference
- `tw-dossier` — cart items
- `tw-ambient` — audio state

The audio system is a clean three-key constellation: theme, commerce, sound. Each persists independently. Each restores independently. The site remembers everything it should and forgets nothing it shouldn't.

## DESIGN PRINCIPLES THAT EMERGED

1. **The cream is non-negotiable.** Pure white kills the entire aesthetic. Every visual choice reads as digital and corporate the moment pure white enters the palette.

2. **The deadpan is load-bearing.** Every page, every sentence, every error message holds the voice. The satire works *because* nothing winks.

3. **The fig.1 / fig.2 move is the brand's signature.** Patent-style diagrams with hairline leader lines and mono callouts signal *document*, not advertisement. This is what separates the site from generic mid-century pastiche.

4. **Monospace for everything numeric.** This is the credibility move. Model numbers, prices, dimensions, dates, addresses — all mono. It's what sells the "this is real equipment" lie at glance speed.

5. **Asymmetry is the rule.** Centred symmetric layouts are forbidden except for the wordmark. Product compositions are always asymmetric — text on one side, image on the other, never balanced in mass.

6. **The succession lineage is the world-building load-bearer.** Every current product has a predecessor with its own dates, going back to the founding. *Service parts remain available for all generations.* This is the move that makes the world feel real.

7. **The cadmium orange is held in reserve.** It only appears as a regulatory marker — in-stock indicators, the primary configuration button, alarm glyphs. Never decorative. Its scarcity is what gives it weight.

8. **Motion is enhancement, not content.** The static layout works completely without parallax or reveals. The motion sweetens the encounter for users who can perceive it; nothing breaks for users who can't.

9. **The dark mode is the night edition.** Not a different brand. The cream-and-ink discipline still rules — bone becomes background, ink becomes foreground, patina and cadmium orange remain the brand anchors. The toggle is itself a piece of editorial voice, framed as DAY EDITION / NIGHT EDITION.

10. **The shopping flow stays in character.** The cart is the *dossier*. The CTA is *Add to Dossier*. Checkout requests a *Formal Quotation*. The confirmation is a brief acknowledgement letter signed by **Margarethe Holzer**. Every UI element that would normally break the world (cart counter, totals table, validation errors) is reframed in the brand voice. *Calibration · Stuttgart · included* is the deadpan punching through the e-commerce furniture.
