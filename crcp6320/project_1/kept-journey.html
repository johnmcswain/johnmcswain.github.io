Kept

Designing a calm place to keep your word.

CRCP 6320 · Project 1 · Process journal · John McSwain (jimcswain@smu.edu

A record of how this project moved from a single question to a working, shippable prototype — what we framed, what the research said, what we built, and what we deliberately left for later.

The question we settled on

Some people struggle to stick to what matters to them when life gets busy and no one’s checking. How might we make it easier for someone to keep everyday promises to themselves, in the context of a normal day?

Phase 01

Finding the right question

We started with the How Might We format and explored several problem areas before narrowing to the one that felt both meaningful and achievable: the small promises people make to themselves and quietly drop when life gets busy. The goal was a question specific enough to act on, yet open enough that many different designs could answer it.

Phase 02

From solo to together

The app would begin in a private, solo mode and let people invite others in through graded modes. The key insight reframed the whole thing: the modes don’t change what you do — they change who’s present when you mark a promise kept. And presence is always invited, never forced.

The five modes, by how much they expose you

  • SoloJust you. The private default.
  • AnonStrangers working on the same kind of promise — company without identity.
  • WitnessOne trusted person sees a single quiet dot, and can’t comment.
  • ReciprocalTwo people hold each other, symmetrically.
  • CircleA small trusted group with one weekly ritual.
Phase 03

Grounding it in evidence

We wrote a design document to test the idea against research. Conventional to-do apps overwhelm people, collide with hard limits on working memory, and aren’t built for social reinforcement — and most lifestyle apps lose around 70% of users within 100 days. That framed both the problem and the principles.

Design principles

  • One promise at a time. No lists, no dashboard — the constraint is the design.
  • Invited, never forced. The app earns the right to suggest a more social mode.
  • Break = reflect, not punish. A missed promise opens a short, gentle question.
  • Calm by default. No streaks, no shame, no notification noise.

What we leaned on

Cowan’s 4±1 / the Hrair limit Implementation intentions The Zeigarnik effect The observer effect The Köhler effect Self-determination theory Over-justification Calm technology

In the market, Finch became the north star — calm, emotionally warm, and non-punitive — while stakes-driven apps like Beeminder and stickK were the cautionary tale: external pressure can crowd out the very meaning that makes a personal promise worth keeping.

Phase 04

Ten ways in

From one question we brainstormed ten distinct design responses — not ten features, but ten different shapes a solution could take.

  • 01One Promise at a Time
  • 02Morning Vow, Evening Verdict
  • 03The Living Artifact
  • 04Quiet Witness
  • 05The Mutual Pact
  • 06Anonymous solidarity
  • 07The Round
  • 08The Wager
  • 09Implementation intentions
  • 10Break = reflect

The two flagship ideas — most distinctive, hardest to copy — were the Living Artifact (generative feedback instead of streaks) and the Quiet Witness.

Phase 05

The Week 5 submission

We assembled the formal deliverable: a one-paragraph problem statement, the How Might We question, and a clear scope — what was in, and what was deliberately deferred. It was produced as both a Word document and a LaTeX version with a linked reference list, ready for Overleaf.

Phase 06

Building Kept

Then a working, responsive prototype in this Mist palette. Five places to be:

  • WelcomeA simulated, calm sign-in.
  • TodayOne promise, with the Living Artifact breathing behind it.
  • Make oneGuided “When ___, I will ___” phrasing, kept small and today-sized.
  • TimelineKept and missed promises, rendered calmly — never as failure.
  • SettingsLive customization, plus the social modes marked “coming soon.”
Phase 07

Making it move

We turned the prototype into a runnable project, then made the Witness real: a small relay so that marking a promise kept on one device quietly fills a single dot on another — the principle held intact, the witness sees a dot and cannot comment.

Phase 08

Refining the details

We fixed a stretched-dot rendering quirk and a text-encoding bug, then replaced the placeholder tones with a six-mood ambient score that crossfades with the app’s state:

ambient introduction soft piano deep reflection sustained promise quiet realization seamless outro
Phase 09

Shipping it

Finally, we built it as a static site with relative paths so it runs from anywhere, and published it to GitHub Pages — a real link to send, test, and present.
