Section 01 · Findings

Engineering analysis

§1.1 Benchmark: what DVNT proved in antweight

Building DVNT for the 1 lb plastic antweight division was the control experiment. Dedicated products exist for every weight class — battle-ready motors, integrated ESCs, drop-in weapon systems. With basic CAD skills, the design came together around proven parts instead of being contorted around scarce ones.

Compared to three years of Lego builds, the difference was stark: the design work was a thousand times easier, and the fights were far more intense. That ecosystem is exactly why antweight keeps growing.

Test articleDVNT — 1 lb plastic antweight

Design difficultyLow — CAD around known components
Fight intensityHigh — and it shows in event growth
§1.2 Constraint: the Lego electronics bottleneck

Eleven Lego bots taught us the same lesson eleven times: builders work with an extremely limited variety of electronics. Every chassis in the fleet — spinner or wedge, 600 g or heavier — was shaped around the same handful of motors, not around the best fighting concept.

That caps weapon geometry, cramps electrical layouts, and keeps an extremely underrated hobby smaller than it should be.

Root causeNo motors sized for Lego weight classes
EffectLimited weapon geometry & layouts
DownstreamFewer events, fewer builders
§1.3 Proposal: smaller, lighter motors

The program objective: bring the antweight experience to Lego by developing smaller, lighter motors that open up new electrical layouts and new weapon geometries — the designs the current fleet could never attempt.

More viable designs raise fight intensity; intensity drives popularity; popularity means more events, more builders, and more fun.
